Remainder fair-share refunds for groups

Split a partial refund across everyone who paid, exact to the cent, and send each PayPal refund once.

PayPal sandbox only. The league, the families and every amount are made up.

1 The season

Alder Creek Youth Soccer collected registration from 24 families through PayPal: $120 per player, $108 for a second sibling, $60 on a scholarship. The Okafors were refunded in full when they moved.

2 The treasurer's notice

Claude writes down what the notice says, each item with the sentence it came from. Code checks that every quote is a whole sentence of the notice and that every amount is in its quote. Claude never works out anyone's refund.

3 Replies from families

Six families answered the notice. Claude sorts each reply and quotes the sentence that shows what they want. Only one kind of reply can change money: asking the club to keep their share. Nothing here can send a refund anywhere except back to the payment it came from.

Write a reply yourself

Pick a family, write what they might send, and Claude sorts it along with the others. Try one of these, or your own.

4 The refund plan

Each family's share of the pool is in proportion to what counts from their payment. Everyone gets the whole cents of their exact share, and the cents left over go one each to the largest fractions.

5 Approve and send

Each refund carries a PayPal-Request-Id chosen when the plan was made, and the same ID as its custom_id. If the sending process dies after PayPal made a refund but before it was written down, the next run finds that refund on the order and does not send it again.

The sending process stopped before every PayPal answer was saved. Our books can't tell whether was paid.

6 Check against PayPal

Reads every order back from PayPal and compares PayPal's refunds with the ledger, family by family.

7 For comparison: no request IDs

A fresh season with the same 24 families, the same notice, replies and plan, sent without a PayPal-Request-Id and retried after the same crash.
